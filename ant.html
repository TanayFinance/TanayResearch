The call

Anthropic builds the Claude family of AI models and sells them mostly by usage, which made up 79% of nine-month 2026 revenue. Revenue reached $33.2bn in the first nine months of 2026, 13 times the prior-year period, and operating cash flow turned positive at $6.6bn. The November 2026 IPO raised about $104.8bn net, including a concurrent private placement, while selling only 7.4% of the company.

The report initiates with a Buy at $75 or below. That level sits at the $74.7 mid-case value and below the $81 probability-weighted value, leaving a cushion for outcomes that can’t be called. It is an entry price, not a price target, and the $18 low case is a real possibility.

Three theses

  1. Demand is broad, but it renews with every model. Customers above $100K a year grew from about 400 to 11,900 in 18 months, and the top five fell from about 36% to 21% of revenue. Yet models under six months old produced over 80% of Q3 revenue, and sequential growth slowed from 144% to 47%, so growth arrives in model-driven steps.
  2. Compute is the moat and the exposure. Compute fell from 160% of revenue in 2025 to 57% in 2026 across four chip platforms, but each new compute dollar added only $1.25 of quarterly revenue in Q3, down from $6.09 in Q1. A stall test at today’s run-rate leaves about $227bn of commitments above need before 2031, nearly all of the IPO-boosted cash.
  3. The price sits a little below value, with little control for holders. The multiple of run-rate revenue has roughly halved since Series F, and the stock trades at 4.9x mid-case 2028 revenue against a 6.2x peer median. The Founder LLC holds a fixed 50.1% of votes, and large staged lock-up releases begin after FY26 results.

Valuation

A discounted cash flow isn’t used, because the commitments and a note liability that moves with the share price make near-term cash flows too uncertain. Instead, low, mid and high scenarios are valued two ways, a 2028 revenue multiple and a discounted 2030 operating income multiple, then averaged and weighted 25/50/25. The result is $18, $75 and $157 per share, or $81 weighted, alongside history and twelve-peer cross-checks.

Key risks

Commitments$900.9bn committed to compute and infrastructure, $70.7bn of it due within 12 months.
Demand durabilityCustomer checks point to cost discipline, and price cuts took about $0.6bn from Q3 revenue.
LegalA pending D.C. Circuit rehearing on a federal supply-chain designation could swing government sales either way.
Supply of stockAbout 15.5bn locked-up shares sit behind a 0.98bn float, released in stages.
GovernancePublic holders own the economics but little of the control.

What would change the view

More positive if new compute keeps earning back more than its $2.16 average, Q4 revenue reaches the mid-case $20.3bn, and the rehearing goes Anthropic’s way. More cautious if growth drifts toward the stall case or lock-up releases arrive before the next major model.
