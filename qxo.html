The call

QXO buys roofing, insulation, waterproofing and lumber from manufacturers and resells them to contractors through roughly 1,150 branches in North America. Founder Brad Jacobs formed it in 2024 from a small software shell and has since bought Beacon, Kodiak and TopBuild, building an ~$18B revenue distributor in a fragmented, low-margin industry where scale drives cost advantage.

After the ~$17B TopBuild deal closed on July 1, 2026, the stock fell from $18.60 to about $13.50 as 312 million new shares landed with holders who had wanted cash. That selling pressure is finite and says little about the business, which is why the report initiates at BUY with a $22.59 fair value.

Why BUY

  1. The selloff was technical. Index funds, TopBuild holders, merger-arb and convert-arb funds sold regardless of fundamentals.
  2. TopBuild is the cash engine Beacon never had. Its historical 65–75% free-cash-flow conversion covers most fixed charges on its own.
  3. Signs of a cycle bottom. A quiet 2025 storm season and soft housing sit on a depressed base, while recent checks show price increases holding.
  4. Deal discipline. Management has raised its bar for new capital deployment as acquisition multiples crept from ~11x to ~15x.
  5. Underwritten synergies. 16 of Kodiak’s top 20 vendors overlap with Beacon (~$5.3B of shared spend).

Valuation

A 2026–2040 discounted cash flow model at a bottom-up 10.0% WACC (4.57% risk-free rate, 5.00% equity risk premium, 1.47 re-levered beta, 5.03% after-tax cost of debt) and 3.0% terminal growth gives $22.59 per share.

Key risks

LeverageNet debt of $8.4B is ~4x EBITDA against a 3x target, entering a weak housing market.
Earnings declineDeleveraging needs ~20% pro forma EBITDA growth in 2027; estimates have been cut all year.
Sunbelt exposureKodiak is ~70% new construction, concentrated in Florida and Texas.
Synergy timingThe $300M target is weighted to 2028–2030.
Key personStrategy and valuation premium rest on a 69-year-old founder.
