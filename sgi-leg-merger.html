The question

On April 13, 2026, Somnigroup (NYSE: SGI) agreed to buy Leggett & Platt (NYSE: LEG), a long-time supplier, in an all-stock deal worth about $2.5 billion. Each LEG share converts into 0.1455 SGI shares. With a fixed exchange ratio, the deal’s value to LEG holders moves with SGI’s share price, so the gap between that implied value and LEG’s own price tells you what the market believes about the deal.

This study tracks both stocks for seven trading days on each side of the announcement to measure that gap and what it signals.

What the data shows

Implied premium = (0.1455 × SGI close) ÷ LEG close − 1, by trading day relative to announcement. Source: author’s event-study workbook.

  1. Announcement day. LEG rose 12.6% and SGI rose 2.3%, so the implied premium collapsed from 13.7% to 3.3% in a single session. The acquirer trading up on a stock deal is a sign the market approved of the terms.
  2. The week after. The premium kept narrowing, to 1.7% by T+7. A spread this thin leaves arbitrageurs little reward for deal risk, so the market is pricing either a very high chance of closing or the possibility of a better offer.
  3. Why it matters. When a fixed-ratio spread approaches zero or inverts, LEG holders are effectively paying more than the deal offers. That can be a signal that the market expects a competing bid.

Investor relations follow-up

To test the thesis, I spoke with the heads of investor relations at both companies about the merger prospectus and the shrinking premium. Both attributed the move to general market sentiment without a specific explanation, and noted that other analysts had raised the same question.
