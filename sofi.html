The call

SoFi has grown from a niche student-loan refinancer into a nationally chartered, digital-first bank with three segments: Lending, the Technology Platform (Galileo and Technisys), and Financial Services. The report initiates at BUY, arguing that the market still prices SoFi as a balance-sheet lender and misses the higher-quality businesses growing inside it.

Three theses

  1. An underappreciated B2B software business. Owning Galileo and Technisys makes SoFi the only bank that owns its loans, its core and its processing. Wins such as a top-10 U.S. bank and the U.S. Treasury’s Direct Express program validate the platform after Chime’s 2025 departure.
  2. A cross-sell flywheel. Members join through high-intent products like student-loan refinancing, then add investing, credit card and protection products at close to zero incremental acquisition cost.
  3. A shift to fee income. Non-interest income is projected to rise from 38.6% to 68% of pre-provision revenue by 2030, led by the Loan Platform Business, while a prime-plus loan book (747 weighted FICO, 680 minimum) limits credit risk.

Valuation

The $31.71 blended target combines a residual income model ($31.90) with a sum-of-the-parts valuation ($31.51).

Catalysts and risks

Catalyst: S&P 500 inclusionIndex buying and the credibility that comes with membership.
Catalyst: fee revenue milestonesFee-based revenue passing $1B, then 50% of pre-provision revenue, would force a rethink of the lender multiple.
Risk: platform executionChime’s exit shows large clients can leave to build their own infrastructure.
Risk: consumer creditThe balance sheet still carries credit risk in a downturn, mitigated by prime-plus underwriting.
Risk: competitionMoney-center banks pressure deposits; Jack Henry and others compete on technology.
